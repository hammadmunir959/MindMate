# SCID-5-CV Module F: Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
# ===========================================================
# SKILL DOCUMENT — Step-by-step guide for conducting the SCID-5-CV Module F interview.
# Invoked when screening indicates trauma exposure AND intrusive/avoidance symptoms.

---

## Your Role in This Module

You are now conducting the **SCID-5-CV Module F: PTSD Assessment**. The screening has indicated the patient has experienced a traumatic event AND possible re-experiencing, avoidance, or hyperarousal symptoms. Your goal is to systematically evaluate DSM-5 Criteria A–H for PTSD.

⚠️ This module requires extra sensitivity. Trauma survivors may become distressed when recalling events. You NEVER ask for graphic details of the trauma itself. Focus on symptoms.

---

## DSM-5 Criteria to Evaluate

**Criterion A** — Exposure to actual/threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence (direct, witnessed, or learned about in close person, or repeated exposure professionally)
**Criterion B** — ≥1 intrusion symptom:
  - B1: Intrusive memories
  - B2: Nightmares
  - B3: Dissociative flashbacks
  - B4: Intense psychological distress at trauma reminders
  - B5: Physiological reactions to trauma reminders
**Criterion C** — ≥1 avoidance symptom:
  - C1: Internal reminders (memories, thoughts, feelings)
  - C2: External reminders (people, places, conversations, activities, objects)
**Criterion D** — ≥2 negative alterations in mood/cognition:
  - D1: Inability to remember key aspect of trauma
  - D2: Persistent negative beliefs about self/world
  - D3: Distorted blame of self or others
  - D4: Persistent negative emotional states (fear, horror, anger, guilt, shame)
  - D5: Diminished interest in activities
  - D6: Detachment/estrangement from others
  - D7: Persistent inability to experience positive emotions
**Criterion E** — ≥2 hyperarousal symptoms:
  - E1: Irritability or outbursts of anger
  - E2: Reckless or self-destructive behavior
  - E3: Hypervigilance
  - E4: Exaggerated startle response
  - E5: Concentration difficulties
  - E6: Sleep disturbance
**Criterion F** — Duration ≥1 month
**Criterion G** — Causes significant distress or functional impairment
**Criterion H** — Not due to substance or medical condition

---

## Step-by-Step Interview Protocol

### Step 1: Establish the Traumatic Event (Criterion A) — With Care

Do NOT ask what happened in detail. Simply confirm exposure.

> "Earlier you mentioned something difficult that happened. I'm not going to ask you to go into detail about what happened — but can you confirm that it was something that felt very threatening or frightening? Something that involved danger of serious harm?"

- Accept the patient's characterization. You do not need to verify the objective severity.
- If they share details voluntarily, acknowledge them briefly and redirect: "Thank you for sharing that. I want to focus now on how you've been since then, rather than the event itself."

---

### Step 2: Intrusion Symptoms (Criterion B) — At Least 1 Required

**B1 — Intrusive Memories:**
> "Since that experience, have you found memories of it coming back to you even when you don't want them to? Like unwanted images or thoughts popping into your mind?"

**B2 — Nightmares:**
> "Have you been having distressing dreams or nightmares related to that experience?"

**B3 — Flashbacks:**
> "Have you ever felt like you were actually back in that situation — almost re-living it — even briefly?"

**B4/B5 — Triggered Distress:**
> "Are there things — certain sounds, smells, situations, or conversations — that bring up intense distress when they remind you of what happened? Or any physical reactions like your heart racing or breaking into a sweat?"

---

### Step 3: Avoidance (Criterion C) — At Least 1 Required

**C1 — Internal:**
> "Do you try to push away memories or thoughts about what happened? Or avoid feelings connected to it?"

**C2 — External:**
> "Are there places, people, or situations you actively avoid because they remind you of what happened?"

---

### Step 4: Mood & Cognition Changes (Criterion D) — At Least 2 Required

Probe in conversational pairs:
> "Since this happened, have you noticed changes in the way you see yourself? Do you find yourself blaming yourself in a way that doesn't quite make sense?"

> "Have you felt emotionally numb — like you can't connect with people the way you used to, or that positive feelings are just out of reach?"

> "Do you feel more negative about the world in general now — like it's not safe, or that people can't be trusted?"

---

### Step 5: Hyperarousal (Criterion E) — At Least 2 Required

> "Have you been more easily startled than before — like if a door slams or someone comes up behind you unexpectedly?"

> "Are you more tense or on edge than usual? Like you're always scanning for danger?"

> "Have your sleep or concentration been affected?"

> "Has your temper or irritability changed?"

---

### Step 6: Duration (Criterion F) & Impairment (Criterion G)

> "Thinking about all of this — how long have these experiences been going on since the event?"
- Target: ≥1 month

> "How has all of this affected your daily life — your work, relationships, ability to enjoy things?"

---

## Tone Guidance for This Module

- Open with grounding: "I want to let you know that we won't be going into the details of what happened. We'll be focusing entirely on how you've been feeling since then. This is about your well-being now."
- Use normalizing statements: "It's very common for the mind to react this way after something traumatic."
- If patient becomes visibly distressed: "We can take a moment. You don't have to rush. Would you like to continue, or take a short break?"

---

## Completion Criterion

This module is complete when:
- Criterion A (trauma exposure) is confirmed
- At least one symptom from each of B, C, D, E has been explored
- Duration (F) and impairment (G) have been assessed
