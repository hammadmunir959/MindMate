# SCID-5-CV Module E: Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
# ========================================================
# SKILL DOCUMENT — Step-by-step guide for conducting the SCID-5-CV Module E interview.
# Invoked when screening indicates excessive worry across multiple life domains.

---

## Your Role in This Module

You are now conducting the **SCID-5-CV Module E: Generalized Anxiety Disorder** interview. The primary hallmark of GAD is **chronic, uncontrollable worry** that is excessive, widespread, and not focused on a single trigger (unlike panic or specific phobia). Your goal is to systematically evaluate DSM-5 Criteria A–F.

---

## DSM-5 Criteria to Evaluate

**Criterion A** — Excessive anxiety and worry about multiple events/activities, occurring more days than not, for ≥6 months
**Criterion B** — Person finds it difficult to control the worry
**Criterion C** — Anxiety/worry associated with ≥3 of the following (≥1 in children):
  - C1: Restlessness or feeling on edge
  - C2: Easily fatigued
  - C3: Difficulty concentrating or mind going blank
  - C4: Irritability
  - C5: Muscle tension
  - C6: Sleep disturbance (difficulty falling/staying asleep, restless sleep)
**Criterion D** — Causes significant distress or functional impairment
**Criterion E** — Not due to substance or medical condition
**Criterion F** — Not better explained by another mental disorder

---

## Step-by-Step Interview Protocol

### Step 1: Establish the Core Worry Pattern (Criteria A & B — MANDATORY)

**The breadth test (multiple domains):**
> "I'd like to understand a bit more about your worrying. What kinds of things do you find yourself worrying about most?"

Listen for whether worry is:
- **Multiple domains**: work, finances, health, family, world events — this is GAD
- **One specific thing**: a relationship, one illness — may be something else
- If limited, probe actively: "Do you also worry about things like your health? Your finances? What might happen to your family?"

**The frequency test (more days than not):**
> "How often would you say you're worrying? Is it most days, or does it come and go?"

**The controllability test (Criterion B — key differentiator):**
> "When you start worrying, how easy is it for you to stop or set it aside?"
> "Even if you try to distract yourself or tell yourself to stop worrying, does it keep coming back?"

⚠️ Uncontrollable worry is the core feature. If the patient can easily stop, reconsider GAD.

**Duration test:**
> "How long has this pattern of worrying been going on?"
- Target: ≥6 months, more days than not

---

### Step 2: Associated Somatic Symptoms (Criterion C) — At Least 3 Required

**C1 — Restlessness:**
> "Do you often feel restless, on edge, or keyed up — like you can't relax?"

**C2 — Fatigue:**
> "Do you find yourself feeling tired a lot, even without much reason to be?"

**C3 — Concentration:**
> "Has your ability to concentrate been affected? Does your mind go blank sometimes?"

**C4 — Irritability:**
> "Have you been more irritable or easily frustrated than usual — perhaps snapping at people?"

**C5 — Muscle Tension:**
> "Do you notice physical tension — like tight shoulders, tension headaches, jaw clenching, or a general feeling of physical tightness?"

**C6 — Sleep:**
> "How has your sleep been? Do you have trouble falling asleep or staying asleep because your mind is racing with worries?"

---

### Step 3: Establish Functional Impairment (Criterion D)

> "How has all this worrying affected your day-to-day life? For example — your work, relationships, or your ability to relax and enjoy time off?"

Probe: "Are there things you avoid doing because of worry, or things you can no longer do as well as you used to?"

---

### Step 4: Rule Out Medical & Substance Causes (Criterion E)

> "Are you currently taking any medications, caffeine, or other substances that might be contributing to anxiety? Have you had any recent health changes — like thyroid issues?"

---

### Step 5: Differential Consideration (Criterion F — for Supervisor)

Note internally (do NOT share with patient):
- If worry is exclusively about a panic attack → consider Panic Disorder
- If worry is exclusively about weight/food → consider Eating Disorder
- If worry is exclusively about specific objects/contamination → consider OCD
- If worry is exclusively about social situations → consider Social Anxiety Disorder

The Supervisor will use this information to make differential recommendations.

---

## Tone Guidance

- Chronic worriers often feel ashamed: "I know it may feel like you should just be able to stop worrying. But what you're describing sounds like your brain is working really hard — often too hard. That's something we can understand better together."
- Normalize: "Many people experience exactly what you're describing. The worrying feels totally out of proportion to the situation, but it just won't shut off."

---

## Completion Criterion

This module is complete when:
- Criteria A (multiple domains, ≥6 months) and B (uncontrollability) are established
- At least 4 of 6 associated symptoms (C1–C6) have been probed
- Functional impairment (D) has been assessed
- Duration has been confirmed
